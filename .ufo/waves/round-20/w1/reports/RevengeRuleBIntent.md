# RevengeRuleBIntent — Rule B was documented as a lead but serialized as an ordinary finding

## Reconstructed contract

- **Round 18 (`TwMissHelperFn`) did not define a “lead.”** Its stated intent was the narrow, same-text case: classify a helper body only when the definition and later call are both visible in the command text and the call supplies literal arguments. The author explicitly kept cross-text helpers out of scope; the real `MadoR13.jsonl:470` call remained clean because its `run_record` definition was in an earlier cell (`.ufo/waves/round-18/w1/reports/TwMissHelperFn.md:3-6,21-47,74-91`). This is visible-body/static-path evidence, not a guarantee that a command completed at runtime.
- **Round 19 (`TwHelperFn`) added a distinct, weaker signal to reach that known miss.** Its rule B treated a literal positional `['prog', ...]` list passed to an unresolved plain-name callee as a *lead*: the argv is explicit, but the callee body is unavailable, so whether it executes the command is unknown. The author knowingly allowed a helper that only formats or displays the list to be reported; `PURE_ARG_CALLS` was a limited exception, not proof of execution (`.ufo/waves/round-19/w1/reports/TwHelperFn.md:9-16,41-45,153-160`). The draft patch describes that same uncertainty at lines 22-27 and 330-355.
- **TwMerge-2 preserved that intended heuristic, but not a machine-readable distinction.** The merge report calls it a heuristic and says that even a helper which only formats/logs argv is reported (`.ufo/waves/round-19/w1/reports/TwMerge.md:36-41`). It states that the lead rule was carried verbatim and that a defined helper with unbindable arguments gets no lead (`:61-73`). Its one post-merge wording fix addressed imported helpers, not the output representation (`:100-103`).

## Where the marker went missing

It was absent from the implementation path, not removed by a later renderer. In the final tool, `helper_argv_leads()` turns each accepted literal list into `classify_shell(...)` results (`tools/mado-tripwire.py:1379-1390`); `spawn_scan()` invokes it for the unresolved-name case (`:1393-1404,1430-1434`). Those results share the ordinary four-value hit shape. The scanner then serializes every hit through one generic loop into `file`, `line`, `tool_call_id`, `timestamp`, `tool`, `class`, `rule`, `argv0`, and `command` fields—there is no `evidence`/`basis` field or branch preserving “lead” (`:1591-1602`). Thus the word *lead* lived in documentation and the helper name, but not in the finding data. The integrated tool SHA-256 is reported as `367be94773d8fec5e6df91f80ac777070c407c1128f7c75b3dcaa26cc83fdb4d` (`.ufo/waves/round-19/w1/reports/TwMerge.md:8-10`).

**[INFERENCE]** The causal gap was the mismatch between the rule’s prose contract and the existing classifier/output contract: rule B reused the existing observed-hit representation to achieve the extra frozen recall hit, and merge retained it without adding an evidence-kind field. The sources show the gap and the merge choice; they do not establish which individual author noticed the schema consequence before review.

## Which review saw it

`CriticC3W2` saw and stated the defect explicitly. It found that a rule-B row had the same keys/values as a proven execution, despite the docstring saying “lead,” and recommended marking it (for example, `"basis": "lead"`) or otherwise differentiating it (`.ufo/waves/round-19/w2/reports/CriticC3W2.md:150-158,182-188`). Its ruling was not to reject the idea categorically: Rule B could be acceptable as a lead heuristic **only with corrections**; as landed, rows could not be distinguished from proven executions (`:30-32`). It also measured 12/12 synthetic data-only shapes triggering under the then-current tool (`:122-148`).

The lesson records this as a **survivor**, not a resolved issue: M14 says lead rows were not marked, cites `CriticC3W2`, and routes lead tagging/name-binding work to round 20 (`corpus/lessons/round-19.md:89`). The later verifier spotcheck independently made the output defect concrete: a `frozenset(['pacman','-Q','hyprland'])` data-only fixture was clean at `9c54c56` but became an ordinary `package_query/pacman:-Q` finding at `b435568`, “without a lead marker” (`corpus/lessons/round-19-verifier-spotcheck.md:67-81`). That spotcheck establishes the precision divergence; it did not execute the fixture code.
