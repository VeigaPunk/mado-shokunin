wrapper prime (/oracle/prime.sh holds the namespace): stdout='foot\n'/rc=0/cache=complete(+0.5s=present); stdout='foot\n'/rc=0/cache=complete(+0.5s=present); stdout='foot\n'/rc=0/cache=complete(+0.5s=present)

The round-47 argv uses --unshare-all, so the resolver is PID 1 of a new PID namespace; the kernel
kills the rest of the namespace when PID 1 exits, so its background `save_cache "$1" &` never
writes a cache. The wrapper keeps PID 1 (`/bin/bash`) alive until the cache file is complete.
A v2 counter must prime this way (or equivalently hold the namespace open); otherwise the warm
path of the pin is unreachable inside the frozen sandbox and every warm fixture would be judged
against a cold oracle.
