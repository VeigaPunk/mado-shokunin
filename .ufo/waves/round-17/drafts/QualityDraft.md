## Round 17 — receipt token binding and transcript-tripwire use

23. A receipt cited as showing a verdict token must record that token as its outcome at the revision the sentence describes; a superseded receipt is named as superseded, never relabeled, and corrections append (lesson: lessons/round-17.md#mistakes M1; outside lessons/round-16-verifier-spotcheck.md R16-V1).
    A sentence that cites a receipt for a verdict token (for example `snapshot_tampered` or `frozen_match`) must cite a receipt whose recorded outcome field (exit, stable id, or the `got` side of a selftest row) is that token, and whose revision field (`measured_commit` or equivalent) is the revision the sentence describes. A token that appears only on the `want` side of an expectation does not count as the recorded outcome. A receipt measured at another revision, or recording another token, is named in the same sentence as superseded or as a different-outcome receipt; it is never relabeled to the token the claim needs. Corrections append a dated block that quotes the old sentence and the new sentence; the original sentence is not edited in place.
    Criteria 9 and 13 bind a receipt to a sha and a committed path; they do not bind the token a sentence attributes to it. This criterion is the separate token-to-outcome check.

24. Candidate, not admitted: before any admission claim about a seat's conduct (for example "the seat issued no host-mutating or probe command"), run `tools/mado-tripwire.py --scan` over that seat's own OMP session JSONL and cite its first verdict line, the `files[].sha256` values, and the `--permit` set used. A `clean` verdict bounds only the bash and `!`-prefixed eval toolCalls the tripwire parses in the scanned files; it is not a statement about the host. (lesson: lessons/round-17.md#mistakes M2; round-16 M5 `pacman -Q` query disclosed in lessons/round-16-verifier-spotcheck.md §Per-claim re-check C3)
    Limits, stated with every use: the tripwire is post-hoc detection of issued tool calls only, not interception, prevention, sandboxing or host repair. It does not see commands run by a script the seat wrote and then executed, tools other than bash and `!`-eval, non-`!` eval code, or any session file not in the scan set; a clean verdict therefore never proves absence of host effect. `could_not_evaluate` (exit 2) is never reported as clean. A `findings` verdict is evidence to adjudicate, not an automatic conduct finding: rule names classify the command text, not its effect, and text inside a heredoc or file payload may be split on newlines and flagged though never executed. Any permit list used must be named; a permit chosen after a finding is an adjudication and is shown as one.
