# W1 residual rulings — frozen predicates

Scope: assess alleged residuals against the exact C1/C2 sentences in `.ufo/waves/round-51/claims.md`; do not amend their meaning. A counter can meet its literal predicate while the predicate remains an imperfect proxy for capability/coverage.

## C1 — tool-ledger capability

1. **Comment-only pointer — PASS, bounded limitation, not a counterexample to the frozen sentence.** Claims lines 28–30 require an existing repo file containing the basename, not an affirmative accountability statement. W1 `RevLedgerPred/attacks.md:5-13` exhibits a comment containing the basename and accurately identifies this literal pass. This weakens the accountability semantics but does not falsify the declared lexical requirement.
2. **Generic/repeated descriptions — PASS, bounded limitation.** Claims lines 19 and 28–30 require a non-empty description; they do not require uniqueness or behavior-specific accuracy. W1 `RevLedgerPred/attacks.md:15-23` shows identical non-empty descriptions satisfy that condition. This does not falsify the frozen criterion; it demonstrates that it measures presence, not useful documentation quality.
3. **Worktree versus revision objects — concrete risk, but not established as a current counter failure by the cited attack.** Claims lines 25–32 specify `--rev`, population “at <rev>,” and an expected base MISS. W1 `RevLedgerPred/attacks.md:45-53` offers a mock possibility, not an observed execution against the frozen counter. If the counter actually reads worktree state and reports TARGET_MET for base while the ledger exists only in the worktree, it falsifies the explicit base behavior. Until such a reproduction, this is an unresolved verification risk, not a proven miss. The required base receipt is a relevant control, but its reported result alone does not establish behavior under a deliberately divergent worktree.

## C2 — sourced dispatcher-dispatch entry

4. **Generic held tokens — PASS, bounded limitation.** Claims lines 61–63 require three distinct citations whose named base lines contain each citation's pinned key token; they do not constrain token specificity or map tokens to entry propositions. W1 `CritHeldFacts/held-attacks.md:3-17` gives three extant lines with token `[the]`, satisfying the stated lexical condition while providing no meaningful support. This is a real false-positive for semantic grounding, but not a falsification of clause (d) as frozen; semantic support is not its declared predicate.
5. **Candidate-bearing seat reports omitted — no demonstrated falsification; bounded baseline scope.** Claims lines 59–61 expressly compare against base knowledge files and files under `round-*/candidate/` recursively, not all wave reports. W1 `RevNovelty/novelty-audit.md:18-28` identifies possible prior candidate-bearing text outside those directories, but does not show that the measured entry exceeds 30% against a specific omitted report or that such reports belong to the frozen comparison set. Thus omission is not a miss against clause (c); it is a limitation in the chosen baseline. If a report is itself in-scope candidate material under the wording and a counter omits it, then that would be a concrete implementation mismatch, not shown by this evidence.

## Overall adjudication

The four lexical/proxy weaknesses (comment pointers, generic descriptions, `[the]` tokens, and omitted non-candidate-directory reports) are disclosed bounded limitations rather than contradictions of the exact frozen predicates. The worktree/revision concern is the only attack that could directly contradict an explicit frozen outcome, but the W1 record cited here presents a hypothetical, not a reproduced counter failure. No frozen predicate or gate is changed by these rulings.
