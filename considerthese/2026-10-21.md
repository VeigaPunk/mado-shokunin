## Top 3 lessons / gains

### 1. Three rejections in a row: the lane has outgrown its own tripwire-tuning loop (rounds 19–21)

From round 19 on, almost every claim was about `tools/mado-tripwire.py`, a static classifier of commands in agent transcripts. Each round fixed the last outside verdict and opened new holes. Round 19's recall gain (3/6 → 6/6) was explicitly "overfit-prone by construction" (lesson M7) and its rule B misread pure data. Round 20's precision repair lost 52 real findings, flipped 13 transcripts to `RecursionError` and took ~N⁴ time before attempt 2 repaired those guards, and even then the final reviewers found 18 new false leads, a missed `imaplib.IMAP4_stream` process and >1,800 MiB memory use (SCORECARD round 20, "Final residual-review disposition"). Round 21 reached 35/35 on a sealed oracle and still failed review on capture lifetime, global leakage, returned-name rebinding and direct `Thread.run` (`residual/FinalBindingReview.md`).

The lesson the lane itself recorded is the right one: a finite scored suite, even one sealed before implementation, does not establish no-harm for a detector whose input space is arbitrary Python and shell. The useful outputs are the guards (removal-side corpus diff, frozen adversarial populations, resource caps) and the honest stops, not the scores. Precision under unchanged historical wants actually went 172/175 → 166/175. The coordinator stopped and asked the operator before a fourth try, which is the correct move. This is now João's call.
