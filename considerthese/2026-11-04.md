## Top 3 lessons / gains

### 1. The bar-footprint chain is the real desktop gain: one tool grew from a measurement to a model of how Hyprland reserves space for waybar (rounds 77–81)

`tools/mado-barfoot.py` started in r77 as a join between the declared waybar box and the live layer and the monitor's `reserved` band. Each round then added one mechanism it had previously refused, and each time a frozen case set was scored against the previous round's tool: r78 margins 18/18 (held model 8/18), r79 static `addreserved` 23/23 (held 18/23), r80 a second exclusive surface 33/33 (held 23/33, and a parser-stub skip also 23/33), r81 rounding and the auto zone 39/39 (held 33/39) with 28 mutants killed (all per `receipts/measurement-c1-r7{8,9}…r81.txt`). The mechanisms are pinned to sources: S478/S479/S480 are hyprutils `CBox::round`, Hyprland's `HYPRUTILS_MINIMUM_VERSION 0.14.0`, and the installed 0.14.2, byte-identical to 0.14.0 for that file. r81 corrects an earlier model on a concrete case: a panel one pixel wider than its bounds lands at `x=-1, w=1002`, where the r80 floor-divided offset gave `0/1001`. The host join on João's DP-1 (3440x1440, waybar at `0,0,3440,26`, `band=26 expected=26`) is CLEAN in `receipts/live-join-r81.txt`, with monitor serial and make/model redacted before capture. The lasting lesson is that the "held model scores lower on the frozen set" control stops the lane from claiming a gain that did nothing, and this chain is where it earned its keep on a real user-facing question (why a bar reserves the pixels it does).
